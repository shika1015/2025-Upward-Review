Lans — Leadership Investigation Report

Based on interviews with 5 direct reports (VPs) · Updated: March 13, 2026

Context

Why this report exists

Lans received one of the lowest scores in the January 2026 upward review (26.67 / 45, 59% achievement), with a particularly low Q3 Communication score of 1.67. A separate structured investigation — comprising individual interviews with all 5 direct VP reports — was subsequently conducted to understand the root causes.

Overall Finding

A consistent pattern emerged across all interviews: teams have learned to function independently of executive leadership in order to maintain progress. While this has allowed departments to continue operating, it creates inefficiencies, communication gaps, and leadership bottlenecks that affect coordination and team development.

Proposed direction

The feedback suggests that additional dedicated operational leadership capacity could significantly improve responsiveness, mentorship, and alignment across teams — while allowing existing executive leadership to focus on other high-priority responsibilities.

Detailed Findings

1. Limited availability and slow communication  5 / 5

All five interviewees described difficulty reaching Lans in a timely manner through normal work channels (Slack and email). While responses occasionally come through other channels (e.g. LINE), communication delays frequently slow decision-making and project progress. Several team members reported needing to follow up repeatedly or wait extended periods for answers or approvals.

2. Lack of regular 1:1s, mentorship, or leadership guidance  4 / 5

Most interviewees indicated that structured 1:1 meetings, mentorship, and leadership guidance have been minimal or absent. Several stated they had either never had regular 1:1s or had only one since assuming significant managerial responsibilities — limiting opportunities for professional development, feedback, and expectation alignment.

3. Operational bottlenecks caused by required approvals  4 / 5

Multiple interviewees described situations where work could not proceed without leadership approval, yet approvals were slow or unavailable. This forced employees to proceed independently in order to maintain progress, increasing the risk of misaligned decisions.

4. Teams frequently operating independently  4 / 5

Due to limited accessibility and guidance, many team members have adapted by solving issues independently or bypassing leadership involvement when possible. While this keeps work moving, it reduces alignment and increases the risk of inconsistent decision-making across departments.

5. Impact on morale and trust in leadership support  4 / 5

Several interviewees described declining confidence that operational concerns would be addressed in a timely or effective manner. As a result, some individuals reported lowering their expectations of leadership involvement and adjusting their work practices accordingly.

6. Managers not fully empowered to lead their teams  3 / 5

Several interviewees indicated that individuals in management roles were not given sufficient authority, guidance, or tools to lead effectively. In some cases, responsibilities expected of those roles were handled directly by Lans instead; in others, managers were criticized for decisions made in the absence of guidance.

7. Limited intervention in personnel or team conflicts  3 / 5

Several interviewees noted situations where interpersonal or performance issues escalated due to a lack of timely leadership involvement. In some cases, requests for guidance or mediation reportedly went unresolved, allowing challenges to persist longer than necessary.

8. Perception that teams lack consistent executive advocacy  2 / 5

Some team members (particularly in Operations) expressed that they do not consistently feel represented or supported at the executive level. This has contributed to frustration around departmental priorities, compensation discussions, and operational decision-making.
